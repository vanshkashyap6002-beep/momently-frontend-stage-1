About CHERMO

Vansh Kashyap founded CHERMO in Delhi, India, to help people turn their photographs and personal messages into a page they can give to someone. A photo on its own might look like an ordinary afternoon. Add the sentence “this was the day you missed the last train to stay a little longer,” and the person opening it knows exactly why you kept it.

What goes into a memory page?

Start with the person who will open the link. For a friend who moved away, you might choose a picture of the table where you always sat. For a birthday, you could put a childhood photograph beside one you took this year. Write down what you remember before you look for more pictures. Sometimes one detail gives the whole page its shape.

Browse the memory page templates and open a preview to see how a design tells its story. Think about which photograph you want someone to see first and which words you would like them to take away. Each template lists its own one-time price in Indian rupees. You can see the price before you decide to order.

How does my story become a page?

Choose your template and submit the names, photos and messages you want to include. Read your message aloud once and check any dates or nicknames. CHERMO prepares the page from those details. Once it is published, you can open the link and send it directly to your recipient. Choose photographs you are comfortable sharing with someone who could forward that link.

What if I just want a photo keepsake?

In the free Memory Studio, you can make a Polaroid, a photobooth strip or a collage and download the result. A single print gives a favourite photograph room for a short caption. A strip works well for a sequence, like the faces you both made just before you started laughing.
